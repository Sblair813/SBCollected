CHAPTER 2
June 1962
The gymnasium at Ephrata High School had been done up with more crepe paper than it could reasonably hold, and by the time the ceremony ended, half of it had drifted loose from the rafters and settled over the folding chairs like blue-and-gold snow.
Kat stood at the edge of the crowd in her white cap and gown, scanning the sea of families for her parents, when Liz plowed into her from the side, nearly knocking the mortarboard clean off her head.
"We did it," Liz said, breathless, her diploma already curling in her tight grip. "Twelve years, Kat. Twelve years and they finally have to let us go."
"You say that like it was a prison sentence."
"Wasn't it?" Liz grinned, looping her arm through Kat's as they let the crowd carry them toward the parking lot.
Out by the gravel drive, the rest of their group had already clustered around the back of Billy's beat-up Ford. Billy had been a fixture in Kat's life for as long as she could remember — their fathers had gone through university together, and the two families had spent enough summer trips and holiday dinners in each other's company over the years that Billy felt less like a family friend and more like an extra cousin. He'd even been her date to senior prom, the two of them going as a favor to each other more than anything else, neither one willing to sit home while the rest of their friends went.
Ruth Anne and Connie were sitting on the trunk, their heels swinging as they tried to untangle the tassels on their caps, while Peggy and Billy took turns passing a contraband pack of Luckies between them, carefully blowing the smoke away from the cluster of parents nearby.
Billy was leaning against the fender, waving his rolled-up diploma in the air the second he spotted them.
"There she is!" Billy shouted over the din of honking cars and shouting families. "The girl least likely to return to Ephrata!"
Ruth Anne laughed, leaning over Connie's shoulder. "Don't start, Billy—she hasn't even taken her gown off yet."
"Bryn Mawr over Harvard" Billy groaned, shaking his head with a theatrical sigh. "Main Line intellectuals. I'm telling you, my old man's already got my life mapped out—he wants me to attend Lancaster Business College. I think he wants me taking over the shop before I even turn twenty."
"Count your blessings," Connie said, blowing a thin plume of smoke over her shoulder. "At least you have a career to step into." Billy shrugged.
"What now, Katherine Pruitt?" Liz chimed in, squeezing Kat's arm as they reached the car. "You've been talking about it since we were eight—pilot school, but you switched it up for history? I say we skip all that and go to Paris"
Kat laughed, but there was a familiar knot behind it. "I don't know that there's a pilot school taking girls, Liz. Not one I could get to, anyway." She shrugged, trying to keep it light as Connie offered her a quiet, sympathetic look. Maybe I'll work at the library through the summer. Save money for our trip, Figure it out."
“You’ll figure it out spectacularly,” Liz said, with the easy confidence of someone who had never doubted her friend for a single day of her life. “Millersville isn’t so far from Bryn Mawr. In the meantime—Raymond Stillwater asked if I’d like to meet him and celebrate at the park later, and you’re coming, because I refuse to celebrate the greatest night of my life without my best friend.”
“Raymond Stillwater?” Kat asked, her brow furrowing.
“Yes! He came into the pharmacy last week while I was working, and we’ve been talking ever since.” Liz squealed.
“Just be careful, Liz. He goes out with a lot of cheerleaders,” Kat said.
“I’m a cheerleader, Kat,” Liz said.
“You know what I mean, Liz.”
Kat glanced back once at the gymnasium doors, at the crepe paper and the flashbulbs and the whole town turned out in its Sunday best, and let herself be pulled toward whatever came next.
That night, Liz drove Kat, Ruth Anne, Peggy, and Connie to the park to meet other classmates to celebrate. "Okay, ladies, it's time to celebrate!" Liz excitedly proclaimed. Getting out of the car Liz threw her arm over Kat hyping up the night, their lives, their future.
“Liz!” a voice called out.
Raymond Stillwater approached, flanked by a few of his teammates. Raymond was the high school’s star running back, responsible for most—if not all—of the team’s touchdowns. He was the local golden boy and easily one of the most charming guys in town. But Kat felt he was no match for Liz, and the last thing she wanted was for her best friend to end up played or hurt by him. “You made it!” Ray said, his face lighting up the moment his eyes landed on Liz.
